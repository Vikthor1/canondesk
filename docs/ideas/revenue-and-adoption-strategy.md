# Veritas Compass — Revenue and Adoption Strategy Ideas

*Version 0.1 — May 2026 | Strategic ideas note — not a business plan or roadmap*

---

## 1. Core Revenue Thesis

Veritas Compass is unlikely to generate meaningful revenue as a low-cost individual subscription app. Its strongest value is as a higher-education AI governance workflow, a CTL and faculty-development training object, and an institutional decision scaffold — the kind of tool a department chair, CTL director, or Faculty Affairs coordinator reaches for when they need to demonstrate that AI use was considered, bounded, and human-reviewed.

That positioning — governance workflow, not productivity widget — is where the revenue potential lives.

---

## 2. Why the App Has Value Despite Being Technically Simple

The app's value is not technical complexity. Its value is making AI-use judgment **visible, teachable, repeatable, and institutionally legible** for non-power users.

The evaluation standard that matters:

> Can a non-expert user produce a useful AI-use decision summary in under five minutes?

If yes, the app earns a place in faculty workshops, onboarding packets, departmental AI-use protocols, and CTL training curricula. That is the adoption foothold. Revenue follows adoption.

---

## 3. Most Realistic Near-Term Revenue Paths

These are the paths with the least friction given the current prototype:

- **CTL and Faculty Affairs workshops** — the app as a live workshop object; facilitator leads a group through one complete Mode 3 scenario during a session on responsible AI use
- **Institutional AI governance training toolkit** — Veritas Compass packaged with a facilitator guide, scenario library, and local policy adaptation as a licensed training product
- **Consulting and local policy adaptation** — advising departments or institutions on how to adapt the governance model and scenario library to their own policies and AI-use contexts
- **Campus or departmental pilot package** — a bounded engagement (one semester, one department, one Faculty Affairs cycle) with structured feedback built in
- **Lightweight licensed web app for academic units** — a hosted or locally deployed version with institution-specific mode vocabulary and scenarios, licensed per department or institution
- **Future SaaS only after workflow validation** — no SaaS architecture before the governance workflow is proven to produce value that stakeholders will renew

---

## 4. Strongest Sellable Package

The app alone is not the whole product. The stronger package is:

- Veritas Compass web app (hosted or licensed)
- Faculty-development workshop (facilitated or self-directed)
- Facilitator guide and slide deck
- Local policy adaptation session
- Mode 3 scenario library (Faculty Affairs, CTL, administrative)
- AI-use decision summary workflow
- AI-safe packet guidance documentation
- Implementation consulting (one or two sessions)

Institutions are not buying a URL. They are buying a structured process for helping their people make better AI-use decisions — and the documentation to show they did.

---

## 5. Revenue and Adoption Risks

- **The app produces no useful artifact.** If users finish a session with nothing to share, print, or attach to a governance file, the value evaporates. The exportable decision summary (Patch 7) is not optional for revenue potential — it is the artifact that makes the session legible to stakeholders.
- **The app becomes too abstract or diagram-like.** A governance layer map with no routing output is a training tool at best, a shrug at worst. The router and result screen are what make it actionable.
- **The app processes sensitive data.** Any document upload or live AI sanitization in the public prototype would trigger procurement, security, and legal review at most institutions — blocking adoption before it starts.
- **The app overclaims compliance.** Implying that a routing result certifies FERPA compliance or AI-safe use would expose users to false confidence and the product to legal and institutional backlash.
- **The app tries to become a live multi-LLM platform too early.** That is a different product with a different procurement path. Building toward it before the governance workflow is validated wastes the prototype's current advantage: it is fast, simple, and requires no IT approval to use.
- **The app is marketed as software alone.** Selling a static web app to academic institutions without the surrounding training and facilitation layer is competing on features. Competing on governance judgment and faculty-development integration is a stronger and less crowded position.

---

## 6. Product Direction That Increases Revenue Potential

The features most likely to increase revenue potential — in rough order of leverage:

1. **Exportable decision summary** (Patch 7) — the artifact that makes a session worth having; required for institutional governance documentation
2. **AI-use route / Workflow Route** (future) — asking "What are you trying to use AI for?" adds a second axis of judgment and makes the tool more useful for a wider range of tasks
3. **Human-review checklist** — a simple checklist that confirms human review before any AI-assisted output is used; institutionally legible and low-tech
4. **AI-safe packet guidance** — helping users understand how to prepare materials before they enter an AI tool; teachable, documentable, and institution-adaptable
5. **Scenario library for CTL and Faculty Affairs workshops** — mode-specific scenarios that facilitators can run in real workshop settings
6. **Future institution-adaptable AI-Safe Packet Gate architecture** (v1.5+) — browser-local preflight checklist, institution-implemented local sanitizer guidance

Live AI is not the next best revenue feature. The next best revenue feature is **a concrete artifact users can export, discuss, and use in training or governance workflows.**

---

## 7. Future AI-Safe Packet Gate

The AI-Safe Packet Gate is a deferred future module. It may become a meaningful value-add, but only under these conditions:

- It remains browser-local and deterministic in Tier 1 (no AI, no backend, no file upload)
- Any AI-assisted sanitization in Tier 2 or Tier 3 is institution-implemented in a local or institutionally approved environment — not run by the public prototype
- Veritas Compass never certifies compliance, de-identification, legality, or AI safety for any document
- Any cleaned or prepared packet is explicitly labeled as a draft or working packet subject to human review
- The gate is never marketed as a sanitizer or compliance tool

Used carefully, the gate architecture could become a differentiator: institutions that want to move beyond governance guidance into structured packet preparation workflows have a documented path to do so in their own environment, supported by Veritas Compass documentation and prompt templates.

---

## 8. Suggested Adoption Path

A simple phased sequence, not a timeline:

1. **Mid-August prototype demo** — CUNY Faculty Affairs stakeholders and CTL leaders; demonstrate Mode 3 end-to-end; gather structured feedback
2. **Faculty-development workshop pilot** — run one or two workshops using the app as a live object; test whether non-technical users can complete a routing session independently
3. **Scenario customization** — adapt Mode 3 scenarios to specific campus contexts (Faculty Affairs workflows, CTL program review, department governance) based on workshop feedback
4. **Toolkit packaging** — bundle app, workshop, facilitator guide, and local policy adaptation into a structured offering
5. **Lightweight hosted or licensed version** — only if pilot feedback confirms institutional demand and the toolkit is validated

---

## 9. Strategic Bottom Line

Veritas Compass can command revenue if it becomes a trusted **AI governance training and workflow product for higher education** — not merely a static web app. The prototype is the proof of concept. The workshop is the adoption mechanism. The exportable summary is the institutional artifact. The scenario library and facilitation layer are the recurring product. The AI-Safe Packet Gate is a future differentiator, not a v1 feature.

The mid-August demo is not the end of the product. It is the first piece of evidence that the governance model works in practice.
